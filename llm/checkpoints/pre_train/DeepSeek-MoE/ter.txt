1. Saving Checkpoints in FP32 (Industry Standard for Training)

Saving your weights and optimizer states in float32 on the disk is the safest way to train.
• The Master Weights: During training, weights are updated by tiny increments. If you save them in 16-bit, those tiny updates get rounded down to zero (underflow), and your model stops learning. Keeping the "Master Weights" in FP32 protects your model from degrading.
• The Optimizer States: AdamW must run in FP32. If you try to save AdamW states in 16-bit, the math breaks completely.
• The Reality Check: When you see a "Qwen 8B that is only 4GB", that is a deployment/inference file. During the actual training phase inside Alibaba's labs, their training checkpoints were massive FP32 files just like yours!

2. Runtime Computation in BF16 via Autocast (Modern Golden Standard)

Using torch.autocast("cuda", dtype=torch.bfloat16) is exactly how state-of-the-art models are trained today. This is called Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP).
• How it works behind the scenes: PyTorch temporarily clones your weights into bfloat16 right before massive matrix multiplications (like Attention or Linear layers) to make the GPU run at maximum speed and save VRAM.
• Safety: It automatically keeps sensitive operations (like loss calculation, LayerNorm/RMSNorm, and your MoE Router/Gating functions) in float32 so the model doesn't crash or output NaN errors.

Summary of your setup

• Storage (Disk): Full precision (FP32) → High fidelity, no data loss.
• Execution (GPU VRAM): Mixed precision (BF16 + FP32) → Maximum speed, lower VRAM usage.

  ### Why Keeping float32 at First Train is Crucial for Precision

  1. Prevents Gradient Underflow:
  Small learning rates (e.g. 6 × 10⁻⁵) produce tiny weight updates Δw. In 16-bit (bfloat16 or float16), these tiny
  updates can round down to zero, causing the model to stall or fail to learn subtle patterns.
  2. AdamW Optimizer Precision:
  The AdamW optimizer maintains two state buffers (exp_avg and exp_avg_sq) for every parameter in float32. This keeps
  the momentum running averages mathematically stable throughout training.
  3. Best of Both Worlds (Mixed Precision):
      • Math Execution: Matrix multiplications run fast on Tensor Cores in bfloat16 via torch.autocast.
      • Master Weights: Gradients are accumulated into the float32 master copy, preserving full numerical fidelity.
  4. Checkpoints:
  When saved to .pt, both models serialize their weights in float32 so no precision is lost between training and post-
  training.
